How to Fight a Traffic Ticket in Alberta

To dispute an Alberta traffic ticket, follow the response instructions before the deadline printed on it and review the available evidence before choosing how to proceed.

Key facts

  • Use the response deadline printed on the ticket.
  • Paying a ticket is a guilty plea: the conviction and any demerits are added to your driving record.
  • Available disclosure may include officer notes, device records, or video relevant to the allegation.
  • The applicable process and attendance requirements depend on the ticket and court.
  • A dispute may resolve or may proceed to trial, depending on the matter.

What disputing a ticket means

Start with the notice itself. Confirm the alleged offence, response instructions, court office, and deadline printed on the ticket. Keep the original and any records related to the event.

A conviction can add demerits to your driving record. Speeding carries 2 to 6 demerits depending on how far over the limit you were. Suspension starts at 15 demerits for a full licence and 8 for a GDL. The effect depends on the offence and your existing driving record.

How the dispute process works

  • Step 1: Check your deadline. Use the response date printed on the ticket.
  • Step 2: Follow the dispute instructions. File through the channel listed on the ticket, or have an agent assist where representation is permitted.
  • Step 3: Request disclosure. Available material may include officer notes, device records, calibration logs, or video. Review it before choosing how to proceed.
  • Step 4: Resolve or go to trial. The available path depends on the allegation, the evidence, and the court process. At trial, the Crown must prove the offence beyond a reasonable doubt.
