Distracted Driving Tickets in Alberta: Penalties and Options

You can dispute an Alberta distracted driving ticket before the deadline printed on it, and a conviction carries a $390 penalty plus three demerit points.

Key facts

  • The Alberta government lists a $390 penalty and 3 demerit points for distracted driving.
  • The restrictions apply on all Alberta roads, even while a vehicle is stopped at a red light.
  • Hand-held phone use, texting, manual GPS entry, reading, writing, and personal grooming are restricted.
  • Hands-free phone use is allowed when the device is not held and is activated by voice or a single touch.
  • Paying the ticket is a conviction, so follow its instructions before the printed response deadline.

What an Alberta distracted driving ticket means

Alberta's distracted driving rules cover more than texting. They restrict hand-held phones, electronic devices, manual GPS entry, reading, writing, and personal grooming on every public road in the province, including while stopped at a red light.

The prohibited activity can support a charge even when the driver's performance does not otherwise appear affected. If a separate moving violation occurs at the same time, Alberta says a driver may receive a second ticket for that conduct.

How to assess and dispute the charge

Start with the response deadline and instructions printed on the ticket. Preserve any dashcam footage, passenger information, photographs, and a written account of where the vehicle was and what device or activity was involved.

If you dispute the charge, request disclosure and compare the officer's observations with the actual restriction or permitted activity at issue. The viewing position, notes, duration of the observation, and whether the device was held may all help explain what the evidence shows.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many demerit points is distracted driving in Alberta?

Three demerit points. The Government of Alberta also lists a $390 penalty, and the points are applied to the driving record on conviction.

Does Alberta's distracted driving law apply at a red light?

Yes. Alberta's restrictions apply even while a vehicle is stopped at a red light, so holding or using a restricted device there can lead to a ticket.

Is hands-free phone use allowed while driving in Alberta?

Sometimes. Hands-free use is allowed when the phone is not held and is activated by voice or a single touch, subject to the other distracted driving rules.

Can I receive two tickets while driving distracted?

Yes. Alberta says a driver may receive one distracted driving ticket and a separate ticket when a moving violation is committed at the same time.

How do I dispute a distracted driving ticket in Alberta?

Follow the ticket instructions. Act before the printed response deadline, preserve relevant evidence, and request disclosure if you choose to contest the allegation.
