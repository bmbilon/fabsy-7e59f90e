Alberta Speeding Tickets: Demerits, Court and Your Options

An officer-issued Alberta speeding conviction carries two to six demerit points depending on the alleged speed, and speeding at 51 km/h or more over the limit requires a court appearance.

Key facts

  • Two demerit points apply for speeding up to 15 km/h over the limit.
  • Three demerit points apply for speeding 16 to 30 km/h over the limit.
  • Four demerit points apply for speeding 31 to 50 km/h over the limit.
  • Six demerit points apply for speeding 51 km/h or more over the limit, with a mandatory court appearance.

What an Alberta speeding conviction means

The current Alberta Driver's Guide lists four speeding bands: two demerit points up to 15 km/h over, three for 16 to 30 km/h over, four for 31 to 50 km/h over, and six for 51 km/h or more over. The highest band also requires a court appearance.

Demerit points are applied on the conviction date, not the ticket date. They remain on the driving record for two years from the conviction date. A full-licence suspension starts at 15 or more points in a two-year period; a GDL suspension starts at eight or more.

How to respond

Start with the response instructions and deadline printed on the ticket. Alberta's Traffic Ticket Digital Service can be used to pay, ask for time to pay, plead not guilty, ask for a trial date, or ask for a prosecutor review where that action is available.

Before choosing a response, preserve the ticket and any relevant photographs, video, or documents. Request the available disclosure and compare the allegation with the officer notes, device records, location, signs, and circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many demerits can an Alberta speeding ticket carry?

The current Alberta schedule ranges from two demerit points for speeding up to 15 km/h over the limit to six points for speeding 51 km/h or more over the limit.

When is court mandatory for an Alberta speeding ticket?

The current Alberta Driver's Guide states that speeding at 51 km/h or more over the limit requires a court appearance. Follow the instructions printed on the ticket.

When are demerit points added to my record?

Alberta applies demerit points on the conviction date, not the date the ticket was issued. Paying the ticket is one way a conviction date can be created.
