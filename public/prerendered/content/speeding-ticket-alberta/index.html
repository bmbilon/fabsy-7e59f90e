Speeding Tickets in Alberta: What They Cost and How to Fight Them

An officer-issued speeding ticket in Alberta carries two to six demerit points depending on how far over the limit you were, stays visible to insurers for three years after conviction, and can be disputed by pleading not guilty before the deadline printed on the ticket.

Key facts

  • Demerits scale with speed: 2 (up to 15 over), 3 (16 to 30), 4 (31 to 50), 6 (over 50).
  • More than 50 km/h over the limit means a mandatory court appearance.
  • Photo radar speeding tickets carry no demerits and never touch your abstract; officer-issued tickets do.
  • Radar and lidar evidence can be challenged through calibration logs and officer training records in disclosure.
  • Most speeding disputes resolve in a reduced charge or fine without a trial.

What a speeding conviction actually costs

The fine is the visible cost. The conviction is the expensive part: demerits accumulate toward suspension (15 points on a full licence, 8 on a GDL), and your insurer reprices your policy against a three-year conviction window. A mid-band ticket can add $1,000 to $3,000 in premiums over that window, several times the fine itself.

Speed measurement is also more contestable than most drivers assume. Radar and lidar devices require testing and calibration, operators require training, and the disclosure package shows whether both held up on the day of your ticket.

How to dispute a speeding ticket

  • Before the deadline on your ticket, enter a not guilty plea at the court office listed on it, or have an agent file for you.
  • Request disclosure. For speeding, that means the officer's notes plus device records: model, testing, calibration, and operator qualification.
  • Review for weaknesses. Gaps in calibration logs, inconsistent notes, or measurement conditions (traffic density, beam width, distance) create doubt.
  • Resolve or try the matter. A resolution can drop the alleged speed into a lower demerit band or substitute a lesser charge. At trial the Crown proves every element or the ticket dies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many demerits is a speeding ticket in Alberta?

Two demerits up to 15 km/h over, three for 16 to 30 over, four for 31 to 50 over, and six for more than 50 over. More than 50 over also means a mandatory court appearance.

Should I pay my speeding ticket or fight it?

Paying is a conviction: demerits plus a three-year insurance record. If the fine, demerits, or premium impact matters to you, dispute it before the deadline printed on the ticket.

How do I dispute a speeding ticket in Alberta?

Enter a not guilty plea before your response deadline, request disclosure including radar or lidar calibration records, then negotiate with the Crown or proceed to trial. An agent can do all of it on your behalf.

Do photo radar speeding tickets add demerits?

No. Photo radar tickets go to the registered owner, carry no demerits, and do not appear on your driving abstract. Officer-issued speeding tickets do carry demerits.

Can a speeding charge be reduced?

Often, yes. Resolutions commonly reduce the speed alleged, which lowers the fine and the demerit band, or substitute a lesser offence. Disclosure review tells us which lever applies to your case.
