Photo Radar Tickets in Alberta Under the 2025 Rules

Photo radar tickets in Alberta carry no demerit points and never appear on your driving abstract, and since April 1, 2025 photo radar is only legal in school, playground, and construction zones, which gives drivers new grounds to dispute tickets from sites that no longer qualify.

Key facts

  • Photo radar is banned on provincial highways and restricted to school, playground, and construction zones since April 1, 2025.
  • Tickets go to the registered owner: no demerits, no abstract entry, no insurance impact.
  • Speed-on-green intersection enforcement is eliminated; red-light cameras remain.
  • Zone qualification, signage, and device calibration are all contestable in disclosure.
  • The dispute deadline is printed on the ticket. Miss it and the fine stands.

What the 2025 overhaul changed

Alberta's automated traffic enforcement rules changed on April 1, 2025. Photo radar disappeared from provincial highways and connectors and is now confined to school, playground, and construction zones, with municipalities able to seek exemptions only for documented high-collision sites. Roughly 70 percent of former photo radar locations were eliminated. Intersection cameras still catch red-light runners, but ticketing on speed through a green is gone.

For drivers, two things follow. First, a photo radar ticket remains a pure money problem: it names the vehicle owner, not the driver, so no demerits and no insurance consequences. Second, the restricted zone list creates a fresh line of attack: a ticket from a site that does not qualify under the 2025 guideline is vulnerable.

How to dispute a photo radar ticket

  • Check the deadline on the ticket and file your dispute before it.
  • Verify the zone. Was the site actually a school, playground, or construction zone at the time, with required signage in place?
  • Request the evidence. Device type, calibration and testing records, operator logs, and the photographs themselves.
  • Check the vehicle identification. Plate legibility and vehicle match errors happen more than you would think.
  • Resolve or contest. Where the site, signage, or device records do not hold up, fines get reduced or tickets get withdrawn.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is photo radar still legal in Alberta?

Since April 1, 2025, photo radar is restricted to school, playground, and construction zones, and banned on provincial highways and connectors. Red-light cameras remain, but speed-on-green enforcement is eliminated.

Do photo radar tickets affect insurance in Alberta?

No. Photo radar tickets are issued to the registered owner, carry no demerit points, and do not appear on your driving abstract, so insurers never see them.

Can you fight a photo radar ticket in Alberta?

Yes. Dispute it before the deadline printed on the ticket. Defences include improper zone designation under the 2025 rules, signage problems, device and calibration issues, and vehicle identification errors.

Who has to pay a photo radar ticket?

The registered owner of the vehicle, regardless of who was driving. Because no driver is identified, no demerits attach and the ticket stays off any driving record.

Is it worth fighting a photo radar ticket?

It depends on the fine. There are no demerits or insurance consequences, so the fight is purely about the money. For larger fines, zone and device defences under the new rules make disputes worthwhile.

What changed with Alberta photo radar in 2025?

The province ended photo radar on provincial highways and restricted it to school, playground, and construction zones effective April 1, 2025, cutting roughly 70 percent of former sites. Tickets from improperly operated sites are contestable.
