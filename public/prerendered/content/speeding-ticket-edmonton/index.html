Edmonton Speeding Tickets: Demerits and What to Do Next

Edmonton officer-issued speeding tickets use Alberta's provincial demerit schedule, ranging from two to six points depending on the alleged speed.

Published by Fabsy Traffic Ticket Services · Sources checked August 23, 2026 · General information, not legal advice.

Key facts

  • The Alberta speeding schedule applies to officer-issued Edmonton tickets.
  • The schedule rises from two points up to 15 km/h over to six points at 51 km/h or more over.
  • Speeding at 51 km/h or more over the limit requires a court appearance.
  • Use the instructions and response deadline printed on the ticket.

What an Edmonton speeding ticket can mean

For an officer-issued ticket, Alberta's current schedule assigns two demerit points up to 15 km/h over, three for 16 to 30 km/h over, four for 31 to 50 km/h over, and six for 51 km/h or more over. The highest band also requires a court appearance.

Demerit points are applied on the conviction date and remain on the driving record for two years. The existing record matters because Alberta's suspension thresholds differ for fully licensed and GDL drivers.

How to review the ticket

Check whether the ticket was issued by an officer or generated through automated enforcement, because the record consequences and evidence can differ. Confirm the alleged offence, location, response deadline, and instructions printed on the ticket.

Preserve any relevant photographs, dashcam video, or documents. Request the available disclosure and review the officer notes, measurement-device records, location, signs, and conditions before choosing a response through Alberta's current ticket process.

Where to find Edmonton traffic court information

The Edmonton Law Courts are at 1A Sir Winston Churchill Square. The official directory lists Edmonton Traffic separately from the other court divisions. Check the court named on your ticket before contacting an office or planning a visit.

Check the Edmonton Court of Justice location page for current location and contact information. Follow the appearance or response instructions on your own ticket.

Can I respond online without hiring an agent?

Alberta's Traffic Tickets Digital Service offers online ticket-response options, including payment, time-to-pay requests and disputes. The Court of Justice says this service does not handle municipal bylaw tickets. Read the official response instructions and your notice before choosing an option.
