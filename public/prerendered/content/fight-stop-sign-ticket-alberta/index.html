Can You Fight an Alberta Stop Sign Ticket?

An officer-issued conviction for failing to stop at a stop sign carries three demerit points in Alberta, and you can dispute the allegation by following the response instructions before the deadline printed on the ticket.

Key facts

  • A stop-sign conviction carries three demerit points under Alberta's current schedule.
  • Use the response deadline and instructions printed on the ticket.
  • Keep the ticket and any relevant photographs, video, or documents.
  • Request the available disclosure before deciding how to proceed.

What the allegation means

The current Alberta Driver's Guide assigns three demerit points for failing to stop at an intersection controlled by a stop sign. Demerit points are applied on the conviction date and remain on the driving record for two years from that date.

The legal and factual issue is not simply whether the vehicle slowed. Review the exact allegation, intersection layout, stop line, officer position, notes, and any available video or photographs.

How to review and dispute the ticket

Confirm the alleged offence, location, court office, response instructions, and deadline printed on the ticket. Preserve your ticket, written recollection, photographs, dashcam video, and any witness information.

Request the available disclosure and compare it with the intersection layout and your records. Alberta's Traffic Ticket Digital Service supports ticket actions including pleading not guilty, requesting a trial date, and asking for a prosecutor review where available.

What to do next

The right response depends on the allegation, available evidence, driving record, and the practical value of a different outcome. No service can predict the result of an individual matter.
