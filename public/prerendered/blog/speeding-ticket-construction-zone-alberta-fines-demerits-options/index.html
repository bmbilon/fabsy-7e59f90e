    Speeding Ticket in a Construction Zone in Alberta: Fines, Demerits & Your Options

    A construction zone speeding ticket in Alberta carries steeper fines than a standard speeding ticket, and enforcement in these areas has intensified across the province in recent years.

    If you have received one, understanding what you are facing and what you can do about it matters before you decide whether to pay or push back.

    Why Construction Zones Are Treated Differently

    Alberta law treats active construction zones as higher-risk areas. Reduced speed limits apply when workers are present, and in many cases the posted limit drops significantly compared to the surrounding roadway. The rationale is straightforward: workers on foot near moving traffic face serious danger from drivers who do not slow down.

    Enforcement reflects that logic. Police services across Alberta, including RCMP and municipal traffic units, regularly conduct dedicated construction zone blitzes. Fines in these zones are typically higher than equivalent offences on standard roadways, and the combination of fines and demerit points can add up quickly.

    Fines for Speeding in a Construction Zone

    Alberta sets construction zone fine amounts through the Traffic Safety Act and associated regulations. The exact dollar amount depends on how far over the posted limit you were travelling.

    Generally speaking, construction zone fines are meaningfully higher than what you would pay for the same speed on a regular road. Speeding charges at the lower end of the scale still carry noticeable penalties; charges at the higher end, or any ticket involving extreme speeding, bring substantially larger financial consequences.

    Because fine tables are updated periodically, always verify current amounts against the Alberta government's official schedule or by consulting a traffic ticket professional. Paying based on an outdated number is a common and avoidable mistake.

    When Workers Are Present

    Many jurisdictions apply elevated fines specifically when workers are actively present in the zone, as opposed to when the zone is marked but unoccupied. Officers issuing the ticket may note worker presence on the ticket itself, which can affect the applicable fine category. Check your ticket carefully for those details.

    Demerit Points

    Speeding in a construction zone typically results in demerit points recorded against your Alberta driving record, following the same general demerit structure applied to speeding offences province-wide. The number of points assigned usually corresponds to how far over the limit you were travelling.

    Demerits accumulate on your licence. Reaching certain thresholds triggers notices from Alberta Transportation, and continued accumulation can result in suspension proceedings. If you already carry points from earlier tickets, a construction zone speeding ticket can push you closer to those thresholds faster than you might expect.

    For a fuller picture of how demerit points work in Alberta, the province's official driver's guide and Transportation department resources are the most reliable reference.

    Your Options After Receiving the Ticket

    When you receive a construction zone speeding ticket in Alberta, you generally have a few paths available.

    1. Pay the Ticket

    Paying the ticket is the simplest option administratively, but it means accepting the fine in full and the demerit points that come with it. It also creates a record of the conviction, which insurers may factor into your premiums at renewal.

    2. Request a Trial

    You can dispute the ticket by requesting a court date. This is your opportunity to have the charge reviewed. Officers must attend and present their evidence. Charges are sometimes reduced or dismissed at trial for procedural, evidentiary, or factual reasons.

    Alberta tickets typically carry a deadline for responding. Missing that deadline can result in a default conviction, so check the response date printed on your ticket and act before it passes.

    3. Work With a Traffic Ticket Service

    A dedicated traffic ticket service handles the dispute process on your behalf. That means reviewing the ticket, identifying any weaknesses in the case against you, attending court if necessary, and negotiating where possible. For drivers who cannot take time away from work to appear, or who simply want experienced representation, this option is worth considering.

    What Makes Construction Zone Tickets Harder to Ignore

    Beyond the fines and demerits, construction zone tickets carry a few additional practical concerns.

    Insurance impact. Speeding convictions, especially in designated zones, can affect your premiums. The more serious the offence, the more likely your insurer will take notice at renewal.

    Employer consequences. Drivers who operate commercial vehicles or hold jobs that depend on a clean licence face professional risk beyond the financial penalty.

    Cumulative effect. If this is not your first speeding ticket, the combined demerit and record impact is compounded. A single ticket might be manageable; a pattern of tickets creates a much harder situation with the province.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Are construction zone speeding fines higher than regular speeding fines in Alberta?

    Yes. Construction zone fines in Alberta are typically higher than equivalent speeding offences on standard roads, reflecting the added risk to workers. The exact amounts depend on your speed over the limit and current provincial fine schedules.

    Do I get demerit points for a construction zone speeding ticket?

    Yes. Construction zone speeding tickets in Alberta generally carry demerit points, following the same structure as other speeding offences. The number of points typically increases with the severity of the speeding.

    How long do I have to respond to my ticket?

    Alberta traffic tickets include a printed response deadline. Missing it can result in a default conviction without a hearing. Check your ticket immediately and act before that date passes.

    Can a construction zone speeding ticket be fought successfully?

    Will a construction zone ticket affect my insurance?

    It often does. Speeding convictions appear on your driving record, and insurers in Alberta frequently review that record at renewal. A conviction in a construction zone, which typically carries a higher fine category, may be weighted more heavily than a minor standard speeding ticket.
