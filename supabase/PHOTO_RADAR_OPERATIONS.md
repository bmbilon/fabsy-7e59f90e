## Product and payment contract

Intake fields are `ticket_type=photo_radar|officer_issued`, `ticket_type_source=upload|manual|entry|default`, and `registered_owner_on_offence_date=yes|sold_before|stolen` for Photo Radar. Existing clients lacking a type retain the officer-issued path. For owner notices the server derives `order_type=photo_radar`, `review_path=ate`, clears insurance context, and prevents insurance-report and paid insurance-assessment checkout. A sold/stolen answer is a review flag, not an automatic rejection or withdrawal.

OCR extracts `offenceDate` separately from `issueDate`; the printed/mailing date must never silently become an owner notice's offence date. Manual correction remains available. A red-light allegation alone does not identify a camera notice; owner wording, s.160(1) or a mailed automated-notice format is needed for an upload suggestion.

Checkout reads the authorized stored type, not a browser amount or cheaper product flag. The new reservation has `type=photo_radar`, `checkout_kind=photo_radar` and `expected_amount_cents=7900`. Signed webhook validation checks base 7900, GST 395, total 8295, CAD, paid payment mode, no discounts/IIR metadata, client/reservation/session/attempt, and stored product. `activate_photo_radar_checkout` commits payment and the single ATE review in one transaction. Replays neither duplicate the case nor reset a disclosure clock. Existing legacy prices remain valid for their own product types.

The signed PDF selects the product from the stored submission, explicitly authorizes the not-guilty plea, displays CAD 82.95, records offence-date ownership, excludes trial/success fee/IIR, and retains exact original Unicode fields in the existing attachment. Changing signed intake or product classification while a checkout is creating/open/paid is blocked. Separately protected server accounting fields, such as referral payment reconciliation, remain mutable.

Photo Radar launches through the English intake and terms only. Both intake endpoints reject a non-English camera product before saving it; payment reads the authorized **stored** locale and ignores browser locale overrides. SQL checks the saved locale under the same ticket lock before reserving a camera checkout. The PDF renderer also refuses Photo Radar authorization for a non-English product locale. This does not prevent clients from entering original Punjabi, Arabic, Chinese, Hindi or other supported-script names in the English form; the document preserves and shapes those fields unchanged. Existing ordinary officer/report flows retain all seven live locales.

The consent deployment must preserve `generate-consent-form.static_files` in `supabase/config.toml`: the existing Noto TTF/GZip font files and HarfBuzz WASM are bundled with Docker. Do not deploy that function using an asset-free API bundle. The actual hosted pre-release source/assets are backed up outside the repository in the release evidence. ## ATE evidence review and client decisions

The active case routes to `ate_reviews` and the existing `/admin/submissions/:id` screen. `src/lib/ate-review.ts` performs a deterministic pass over structured, referenced evidence; an operator or approved disclosure worker must supply that evidence. It does not read raw disclosure files or invent facts. Eight checks cover plate/image, speed-site permission/exception, zone hours, doubled construction fine conditions, duplicate policy, certificate mailing/service, offence-date ownership, and operator/device records.

Record the **actual** time complete, readable disclosure was received and matched. Entering the review later does not restart the clock. `save_ate_review` sets the due time exactly 48 elapsed hours later and rejects future timestamps or changes to a recorded receipt/action timestamp. A stale staff form cannot erase another operator's action record. An action note alone does not establish that the Crown replied or that a result occurred.

Apply rules using the offence date, device, municipality and site. Red-light cameras do not inherit speed-only site/zone restrictions. Calgary approved speed-on-green exceptions must be checked, not categorically failed. Edmonton's five-minute policy is documented; request a contemporaneous Calgary policy before treating a Calgary duplicate as noncompliant. The guideline's 21 working-day mailing provision is distinct from legal service and actual receipt. Check certificate dates, reissues, postal disruptions and applicable service provisions; a late arrival or missing separate service certificate is not an automatic defence. There is no assumed universal calibration-expiry rule. Primary source references supporting these guardrails include the [2025 ATE guideline](https://secure.reddeer.ca/media/reddeerca/city-services/police-rcmp/2025-ATE-Guideline.pdf), [Calgary April 2026 enforcement locations](https://newsroom.calgary.ca/april-2026-photo-enforcement-locations/), and the [Calgary Police March 2026 commission report](https://www.calgarypolicecommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar-2026-CPC-13.1.1-Bureau-of-Community-Policing.pdf).

`record_ate_crown_offer` stores each exact Crown response as a new immutable version. The client sees it in `/portal/cases/:id` and may approve, decline or ask a question. `respond_to_ate_crown_offer` verifies confirmed account ownership, current version, expiry and decision state under the same case lock used to replace an offer. The UI resets its checkbox/reply on case or offer change. Recording an instruction never transmits an acceptance or enters a plea. An authorized operator still must act on the exact client-approved terms.

`record_ate_outcome` records the original and actual final fine and the final Crown/court record reference. A reduced outcome requires approval of a matching latest offer. An unchanged outcome records zero reduction; a withdrawal records a final fine of zero. A generic case status update cannot mark ATE completed or fabricate its outcome. The outcome form refreshes the parent case status after success.

## Notification / clone handoff

`ate_case_events` is a private, durable queue for `payment_confirmed`, `disclosure_complete`, `action_recorded`, `crown_offer`, `client_instruction` and `outcome_recorded`. Events have unique keys, an audience (`client` or `staff`), a secure portal path and `delivery_policy=prepare_for_operator_review`. The staff review screen displays pending drafts and a copy action. Copying does not create a mailbox draft or send a message.

An approved server worker uses `claim_ate_case_events(p_limit)` (1–50) and `complete_ate_case_event(p_id,p_claim_token,p_delivery_reference)`. Claims have a ten-minute lease and at most five attempts; stale consumers cannot complete another claim. Use `event_key` as the downstream deduplication key. A completion reference must identify the real handoff/delivery action; never mark a failed or unsent notification as delivered. Only the service role can claim/complete, staff can read, and clients cannot read the queue or private evidence. The external dispatcher and raw-disclosure intake must be connected and tested separately. Existing insurance emails are excluded for ATE; any separately implemented staff-triggered final-outcome email must stay photo-aware and must not sell an IIR.

## First-20 measure and local verification

`ate_first_twenty_metrics()` selects the first 20 **paid ATE files**, ordered by payment activation time and ID, not the first 20 successful outcomes. It returns cohort/resolved/pending counts, the median actual fine reduction, `below_40`, and whether all first 20 outcomes are known. Zero reductions stay in the denominator; pending outcomes stay explicitly missing. The staff case list displays this readout and flags a median below CAD 40. Read it as provisional until the full cohort is resolved.

Local checks, with synthetic records only:

```sh
deno test --no-lock --allow-read supabase/functions/_shared/photo-radar.test.ts supabase/functions/_shared/consent-pdf.test.ts

python3 supabase/tests/test_photo_radar_migration.py

```

The Python test initializes a temporary socket-only PostgreSQL cluster, applies the additive migration against a synthetic compatible schema, checks product constraints, no-IIR enforcement, payment replay, client privacy/approval, actual-outcome completion, stale timestamp rejection, outbox permissions/claims, server-accounting compatibility and the zero-outcome median, then destroys the cluster. It never loads Supabase credentials or contacts an existing database. This is evidence of local behavior, not proof that live Stripe, the full production migration chain, or an external worker has been deployed.
