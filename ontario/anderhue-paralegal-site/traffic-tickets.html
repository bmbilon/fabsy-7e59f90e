Your options

Three ways to answer a ticket.

Your offence notice lists them, with a deadline to choose. We explain what each one means for you before you decide.

  1. Pay the fine

    You pay the amount on the notice, and a conviction is registered.

  2. Early resolution meeting

    You meet with the prosecutor to discuss the charge, where the court offers these meetings.

  3. Request a trial

    You ask for a trial, and the charge is decided in court.

Do not let the deadline pass. If you do nothing in time, you can be convicted without a hearing, and costs are added.

Good to know

A few facts about Ontario tickets.

General information, not advice about your ticket. We read your notice before we advise you.

  • Disclosure

    You have a right to disclosure of the evidence against you.

  • Representation

    A licensed paralegal can represent you, with your written authorization.

  • Demerit points

    Demerit points are administered by the Ministry of Transportation and depend on the offence.

  • Camera tickets

    Camera tickets are issued to the vehicle’s owner and carry no demerit points.

Questions

What drivers ask us

Deadlines, the free review, court and outcomes.

How long do I have to respond?

Your offence notice lists the deadline, usually 15 days. Upload your ticket as soon as you can, so there is time to act.

Is the ticket review free?

Yes. We check your ticket and its deadline and explain your options. If you want us to act for you, we quote a fee in writing before you decide.

Do I have to go to court?

A licensed paralegal can represent you, with your written authorization. If your attendance is needed, we will tell you.

Can you tell me how my case will turn out?

No one can promise an outcome. We explain your options and what each one involves, so you can make the decision with the facts in front of you.

What about camera tickets?

Camera tickets are issued to the vehicle’s owner and carry no demerit points. Upload yours and we will check the deadline and your options.
