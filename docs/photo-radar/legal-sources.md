# Photo radar: source and review notes

Reviewed August 31, 2026. These notes support the public copy and the ATE checklist; they are not a finding about a particular ticket. Apply the rules and approvals in force on the **offence date**, identify the procedure used, and retain the evidence behind any Crown request. A missing document is an unanswered question, not proof of noncompliance.

## Owner notices, insurance and scope

- [Calgary Police: Automated Traffic Enforcement](https://www.calgarypolice.ca/public-safety/traffic-and-road-safety/automated-traffic-enforcement.html), current page checked August 31, 2026: the ticketing section confirms registered-owner ATE notices have no demerits, driving-record impact or insurance impact. It also says the ticket cannot be reassigned to another driver. The public service claim is limited to this kind of notice; it does not describe separate charges against an identified driver.
- [Edmonton: Automated Traffic Enforcement](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_safety/automated-enforcement), checked August 31, 2026: explains mailed owner notices, images, s.160(1) and the absence of demerits. The website quotes older statutory wording; the product uses the section reference without reproducing that wording.
- [Traffic Safety Act, Alberta King's Printer copy hosted by the Village of Delburne](https://www.delburne.ca/application/files/3317/4284/9232/Alberta_Traffic_Safety_Act.pdf), ss.160–161, printed pages 167–168: for a moving vehicle, s.160(2) concerns both the owner not driving and the absence of the owner's express or implied consent. “Someone else was driving” is not enough on its own. Sale and theft answers therefore collect supporting evidence and flag review; they do not automatically reject or clear a file. Verify the applicable consolidation before making a legal submission.

## Location, hours and device evidence

- [Alberta: Photo radar in Alberta](https://www.alberta.ca/photo-radar-alberta), checked August 31, 2026: the policy changes took effect April 1, 2025. Default speed-enforcement locations are school, playground and construction zones; the province allows applications for site exemptions. Red-light enforcement is treated separately.
- [2025 Automated Traffic Enforcement Technology Guideline, official Alberta document hosted by Red Deer](https://secure.reddeer.ca/media/reddeerca/city-services/police-rcmp/2025-ATE-Guideline.pdf), dated March 24, 2025, checked August 31, 2026: ss.3.1–3.5, 10.2 and Schedules A/F are relevant. In particular, s.3.3.1 permits designated zones even on provincial highways or connectors. Site approval is a factual check, not a blanket highway defence. Section 10.2 specifies mailing within 21 **working** days of detection. Schedule F uses manufacturer/Ministry testing requirements for mobile units; ISDs have 30-day testing and independent speed-verification requirements. Request the appropriate records; do not assume every device shares a single calibration-expiry rule. Guideline noncompliance does not itself establish an automatic court dismissal.
- [CPS report to the Calgary Police Commission](https://www.calgarypolicecommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar-2026-CPC-13.1.1-Bureau-of-Community-Policing.pdf), dated March 10, 2026, pages 3–4: confirms five approved speed-on-green exemptions. [CPS April enforcement release](https://newsroom.calgary.ca/april-2026-photo-enforcement-locations/), April 1, 2026, confirms speed-on-green operation at five sites. Obtain the actual site approval and effective dates for the file. Do not claim all post-2025 speed-on-green notices are invalid.
- [Edmonton: Schools and Playground Zones](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_safety/schools-playground-zones), checked August 31, 2026: posted playground zones normally use 30 km/h from 7:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily. The page also describes a special flashing-beacon schedule on 118 Avenue. Review the actual sign and timestamp; do not hardcode one daily schedule for every Edmonton location.
- [Calgary: Building safer routes to school](https://www.calgary.ca/roads/safety/be-safe.html), checked August 31, 2026: playground-zone limit 30 km/h, 7:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. every day. School holidays do not turn a playground zone off. Other Alberta municipalities may use different signs and hours.
- [Alberta: Roadside worker safety](https://www.alberta.ca/roadside-worker-safety), checked August 31, 2026: worker presence matters to doubled construction speeding fines. Absence of a worker in a cropped photograph does not prove nobody was present. The base posted construction limit and the doubled-fine question are separate checks.

## Duplicate notices: do not overstate the current evidence

[Edmonton's current enforcement page](https://www.edmonton.ca/transportation/traffic_safety/automated-enforcement) expressly retains the same-vehicle, same-municipality five-minute rule and provides a review contact. Check both notices, not just a client recollection.

The [2023 provincial guideline, official Red Deer copy](https://secure.reddeer.ca/media/reddeerca/city-services/police-rcmp/2023-Automated-Traffic-Enforcement-Technology-Guideline.pdf), page 10, contained a five-minute provision. The reviewed 2025 guideline does **not** repeat it. The current CPS page and 2025 annual report did not establish a continuing Calgary rule. The Calgary public FAQ and checklist therefore ask for both notices and confirmation of the policy applicable on the offence date. Do not present the build-sheet statement “Calgary won't issue multiple notices” as a verified current rule or an automatic defence. Obtain contemporaneous CPS authority before treating that item as established noncompliance.

## March 2026 fines

The [CPS March report](https://www.calgarypolicecommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar-2026-CPC-13.1.1-Bureau-of-Community-Policing.pdf), page 4, confirms selected provincial fine changes effective **March 13, 2026**, determined by offence date. The [King's Printer catalogue](https://www.alberta.ca/laws-online-catalogue) lists the March 13, 2026 specified-penalty edition.

The public pages use only two examples from [Alberta's current speeding table](https://www.alberta.ca/speeding-fines-in-alberta): 20 km/h over is $228; 30 km/h over is $324. Those amounts include the victim surcharge. They are not red-light fine amounts. The [fine-payment guidance](https://www.alberta.ca/fine-payment) distinguishes the regulation's base penalties from surcharge-inclusive listings. Do not add Fabsy's GST to a government fine, or apply current penalties to an earlier offence. No full fine calculator or unverified red-light amount is introduced.

## Mailing and service

The [Provincial Offences Procedure Act, King's Printer copy hosted by MD of Pincher Creek](https://www.mdpinchercreek.ab.ca/docs/files/websiteassets/CPO%20Open%20House/Provincial%20Offences%20Procedure%20Act.pdf) is labelled current as of October 15, 2024. It is a reference for identifying the applicable provisions, **not proof that no later amendment or service order applies**. The current King's Printer endpoints did not return readable legislation during this check; obtain the applicable text/order before making a date-based legal submission.

- Part 3, s.31(5)–(5.1), allows ordinary-mail service for qualifying owner notices and uses the mailing date on the **certificate of offence**. Deemed service is rebuttable: the seventh day for Alberta addresses and the fourteenth for addresses outside Alberta.
- Section 32(5) says a separate service certificate or affidavit is not needed when s.31(5.1) deemed service applies. Its absence alone is not an error.
- Section 4(1)'s twelve-month institution-of-proceedings limit is different from service, a response deadline, or the ATE guideline's working-day mailing requirement.
- Section 35(2) excludes the listed owner speeding/red-light offences from the generic written guilty-with-representations route. Use the authorized Crown process; do not sell an inapplicable shortcut. A not-guilty plea can lead to a trial date even though this product excludes trial representation. Preserve deadline instructions and a clear handoff if it does not resolve.

[CPS's current reissued-ticket notice](https://www.calgarypolice.ca/public-safety/traffic-and-road-safety/automated-traffic-enforcement.html) explains the 2025 postal disruption, court-authorized courier service and reissuance with a covering letter. Collect that history before drawing a service conclusion. The public pages deliberately do not say that late arrival automatically voids a ticket.
