## Propositions requiring Alberta-qualified review

### 1. Alberta traffic-trial evidence article

- **Framework and forum:** Confirm the description of the Provincial Offences Procedure Act, the Alberta Evidence Act, the Traffic Safety Act, applicable Criminal Code summary-conviction provisions, the Alberta Court of Justice, and the role and powers of a justice of the peace. Flag any rule that depends on the charge, court location, mode of trial, or current practice direction.
- **Disclosure:** Confirm that, after a request by the accused or an authorized representative, the prosecution must provide relevant, non-privileged material in its possession or control; that the package varies; that additional device or maintenance records are not automatic; and that disclosure material does not automatically become admissible trial evidence. Confirm the article does not imply that the Crown, police, or court staff must interpret the file or give legal advice.
- **Admissibility and proof:** Confirm the explanations of relevance, authenticity, hearsay and its exceptions, refreshing memory, electronic records, exhibits, continuity, judicial notice, subpoenas, and the difference between weight and admissibility.
- **Certificates and affidavits:** Confirm the circumstances, notice/service requirements, and any right to require attendance or cross-examination for certificate or affidavit evidence, including Provincial Offences Procedure Act s.39 and Traffic Safety Act s.163. Reject any categorical “no officer means no case” proposition.
- **Trial conduct:** Confirm the descriptions of opening/closing submissions, objections, cross-examination, an accused testifying, prior statements, credibility, the presumption of innocence, proof beyond a reasonable doubt, and any use of the *W.(D.)* framework.
- **Onuses and defences:** Confirm the distinction among actus reus, mens rea, strict-liability offences, due diligence, absolute liability, evidentiary burdens, and legal burdens. Identify propositions that cannot safely be generalized across all Alberta traffic charges.
- **Charter and remedies:** Confirm any discussion of Charter notice, delay, disclosure remedies, exclusion of evidence, adjournments, stays, or appeal rights and the procedural prerequisites for each.
- **Representation and tone:** Confirm the scope attributed to a lawyer, authorized agent, independent legal clinic, court staff, and self-represented accused. Confirm the page is informative rather than coercive, does not overstate the danger of self-representation, and does not imply that every person needs paid representation.
- **Source fidelity:** Check every case summary against the cited decision and every statutory proposition against the law in force on the review date. Confirm “NOT LEGAL ADVICE” remains prominent without being treated as a substitute for accuracy.

### 2. Speeding-ticket guide

- **Ticket type and response route:** Confirm the distinctions among officer-issued tickets, registered-owner automated-enforcement notices, summonses, municipal/bylaw matters, and administrative penalties, including which online response options may be available and why the printed notice controls.
- **Demerit bands and numerical claims:** Verify every speed band, fine, surcharge, demerit point, court-appearance requirement, and effective date against the current schedule. In particular, confirm that 51 km/h or more over is described as six demerit points and a required court appearance.
- **Suspension and seizure:** Confirm that Traffic Safety Act s.86 permits a court, on conviction for the identified speeding offence, to suspend a licence for up to three months. Confirm the guide does not state that the 51+ allegation alone automatically creates a roadside suspension or vehicle impoundment. Review s.172 separately before describing racing, a bet or wager, seizure, suspension, or impoundment.
- **Construction-zone consequences:** Confirm any enhanced penalty is limited to the circumstances engaged by Traffic Safety Act s.115(2)(p.1)–(p.2) and the Procedures Regulation Schedule, Parts 32–33. Reject blanket statements that all construction, school, or playground-zone speeding fines are simply “doubled”; verify the people-present, traffic-control-device, offence-date, and applicable-table conditions.
- **Evidence and disclosure:** Confirm the disclosure boundary above and any description of officer evidence, notes, certificates, affidavits, device records, calibration/testing material, service, and trial attendance.
- **Decision guidance:** Confirm the “should I dispute it?” factors and myths are neutral, fact-specific, and do not promise return on investment, withdrawal, reduction, demerit, insurance, or premium results.
- **Scope and call to action:** Confirm statements about self-representation, agent services, lawyer referrals, eligibility, and trial representation are accurate and kept separate from legal-information content.

### 3. Traffic-ticket dispute guide

- **Available paths and deadlines:** Confirm the guide accurately separates payment, not-guilty/trial requests, prosecutor review, negotiated resolution, summons procedure, municipal matters, registered-owner notices, and administrative penalties. The ticket-specific instructions and deadline must control.
- **Prosecutor review versus trial:** Confirm prosecutor review is not described as a trial, cancellation, entitlement to an offer, or guarantee of a reduction or withdrawal. Verify statements about the prosecution's burden at trial and the accused's right to require proof.
- **Disclosure and preparation:** Confirm the disclosure scope, request process, follow-up for missing material, and difference between disclosure and admissible evidence. Reject any claim that a missing item automatically invalidates the ticket.
- **Registered-owner process:** Review Provincial Offences Procedure Act ss.33–35 and Traffic Safety Act ss.160–163 for the owner's plea, dispute, trial, service, certificate, and evidentiary rights. Confirm the page does not collapse owner liability into proof that the registered owner was driving.
- **Officer, certificate, and affidavit evidence:** Confirm the circumstances in which written evidence may be used under Provincial Offences Procedure Act s.39 and Traffic Safety Act s.163, plus any notice or attendance mechanism. Reject categorical claims that an officer's absence requires dismissal.
- **Representation:** Confirm the descriptions and boundaries of self-representation, authorized traffic-ticket agent work, legal advice, lawyer-only work, and matters outside paid-agent scope.
- **Outcomes and commercial claims:** Confirm no outcome, timeline, demerit, insurance, premium, or cost-saving result is promised or implied; verify that service terms are clearly separated from procedural explanations.

### 4. Photo-radar guide

- **Owner liability and procedural rights:** Confirm Traffic Safety Act s.160 owner liability is described accurately and does not imply the owner was the driver. Review ss.160–163 and Provincial Offences Procedure Act ss.33–35 for plea, trial, service, certificate, and evidentiary rights.
- **Demerits and insurance:** Confirm the current Demerit Point Program and Service of Documents Regulation, Schedule 1, assigns no demerit points to an owner conviction under Traffic Safety Act s.160. Confirm the page makes no guarantee about insurer knowledge, underwriting, premiums, renewal, or treatment and distinguishes an identified-driver roadside ticket.
- **Automated-enforcement locations:** Verify the effective date and scope of Alberta's automated traffic enforcement policy; school, playground, and construction-zone rules; red-light-camera treatment; provincial-highway/connector rules; and the availability and effect of approved exceptions. ## Optional translation review and owner-publication controls

These pages do not currently have translated article bodies. If translations are created later, human review remains recommended but is not a publication prerequisite when the owner explicitly authorizes the exact machine-translated version. Publication without review must use a prominent `NOT LEGAL ADVICE` and machine-translation notice, must not identify the copy as reviewed, and must preserve fail-closed source/version controls. The following checklist is required before describing a translation as reviewed:

- [ ] The exact English source version has Alberta-qualified approval and is frozen with its hash, review date, and source-law date.
- [ ] A human translator or translation reviewer competent in the target language and Alberta legal terminology reviews the entire page; raw machine output is not published as reviewed legal information.
- [ ] The translation preserves the English meaning, qualifications, citations, section numbers, ticket-type distinctions, and uncertainty. It neither adds advice nor strengthens an outcome claim.
- [ ] “NOT LEGAL ADVICE,” Alberta jurisdiction, agent/lawyer distinctions, and all deadline/effective-date warnings are conveyed clearly in the target language.
- [ ] Legal names and terms without a safe equivalent are retained or explained consistently; translated labels do not masquerade as official statutory wording.
- [ ] Links and visible anchor text still identify the correct current authority. The reviewer checks accessibility of the underlying source, even if the authority is available only in English.
- [ ] A second human performs rendered-page QA for numbers, negation, dates, links, text direction, truncation, metadata, FAQ/JSON-LD parity, canonical/hreflang behavior, and fallback language.
- [ ] The locale records translator/reviewer identity, competence, dates, source English hash, translation hash/version, disposition, and unresolved limitations.
- [ ] Any material English change automatically marks every translation stale and removes its reviewed status until the affected locale is re-reviewed. A separate owner decision is required to republish or re-index changed machine-translated copy.

If a locale has not passed every review item, do not imply translated legal review. Any owner-authorized machine publication must remain clearly labelled, bind the exact English and translated versions, preserve official-source and deadline caveats, and keep private or incomplete routes out of Search.
